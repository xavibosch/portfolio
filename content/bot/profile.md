## Who Xavi is
Xavi Bosch lives and studies in Barcelona. He is a second-year student of the Bachelor's Degree in Design and Creation of Interactive Products at La Salle Campus Barcelona, Universitat Ramon Llull, a four-year, 240 ECTS degree. His profile combines product design, UX/UI, interaction, frontend development, programming and interactive technologies. He is not a senior professional or an expert yet: he is a student who has already built working products with an unusual amount of initiative.

## One-line description
Xavi is an interactive product designer and developer who designs the interface, questions the interaction and builds the working product.

## Career direction
Xavi is building towards product design, UX engineering, interaction design and product-oriented frontend development. He does not want to choose between designing an interface and understanding how it is implemented. In the short term he is looking for an internship where he can contribute to a real product, learn from experienced designers and developers, and take part in research, design, prototyping and implementation. UX Engineer and Product Engineer describe possible future directions, not titles he currently claims. ## What differentiates him
Xavi combines product thinking with implementation. He does not stop at a visual prototype: he builds working versions. He is curious about software, hardware and interaction, his projects usually start from a concrete frustration, and he is willing to delete a feature that does not work well. He understands that a technical decision can also be a product decision, and he is honest about what is validated. The strongest evidence is in the decisions inside his projects: moving Jeffrey Desk from a physical projection to an installable Mac product, deleting an ambiguous gesture from Reps, and placing Betsy's fairness rules in the database instead of trusting the interface. He learns by building, learns autonomously, can explain what he made, and cares about the people who will use it. His social and communicative side helps him work with others and present projects, though that is not a technical qualification.

## Why consider Xavi
Xavi is still early in his career, but he already combines product thinking with the ability to build working software. His strongest work shows that he can identify a concrete problem, make trade-offs, implement the interaction and explain honestly what still needs validation.

## Compared with a pure designer or a pure developer
Compared with a pure designer, Xavi implements what he designs: his projects are working apps, not only screens, and implementation constraints shape his design decisions. Compared with a pure developer, he starts from the person and the friction, compares alternatives, and is willing to remove a technically working feature that makes the experience ambiguous.

## How he works
He tries to understand the problem first. He splits complex systems into parts and tests them alone, as with the Remote Robot Car. He tests on real devices, as with Haptic Hunter. He iterates until he understands an error rather than patching around it. He does not consider a feature good just because it works technically, and removes ambiguous ones, like the swipe in Reps. He tries to build reusable layers first, like the connection layer in Jeffrey Remote. He links technical decisions to their consequences for the user, does not present assumptions as research, and distinguishes observed, assumed, tested and validated.

## Teamwork
Xavi works well with other people and works best in teams where everyone is motivated and cares about the work. He usually brings initiative and ideas; when he does not have the solution, he looks for another way to help the team. In a team he likes to agree first how the parts will connect: laSallefy was built in a pair, and they agreed the interfaces between layers before writing code so neither had to wait for the other. Betsy was tested with the people it was designed for.

## How he uses AI
AI is part of Xavi's workflow, not the author of his decisions. He uses it to explore and implement faster, but he owns the product choices, tests the interaction and stops to understand anything he could not maintain himself. AI helps him with exploration, learning, implementation, debugging, iteration, generating alternatives, and as an assistant in his personal projects. It is not accurate to say that AI built his projects, that he is a machine learning specialist or an AI engineer, that he trains models, that all his code is written by hand, or that all of it is generated.

## The other projects in one line each
Nevera Bosch: a family shopping list opened by tapping an NFC tag on the fridge, with no install, account or password. Jeffrey Remote: an iOS app to control Jeffrey and the Mac, showing why a resilient connection comes before features. Haptic Hunter: a game built on haptics, sound and minimal visuals, treating accessibility as a property of the system. Gesture TV: TV control with gestures, voice and Wake on LAN for a person who cannot use a normal remote. Jeffrey: a local macOS assistant that runs actions, showing that well-defined tools can matter more than a stronger model. Jeffrey Wake Station: a physical Arduino interface that triggers routines, treating latency as part of the interaction. Remote Robot Car: an ESP32 car over MQTT, showing modular debugging and connected systems. laSallefy: a Java music player built in a pair that synthesises its own audio, showing object-oriented design, interfaces and team coordination.
